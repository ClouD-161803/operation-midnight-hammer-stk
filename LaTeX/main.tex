\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}

This document reports the modelling of the United States Department of War mission codenamed \emph{Operation Midnight Hammer}, which involved aerial strikes on several Iranian nuclear facilities. The operation was modelled using the Ansys software package Systems Tool Kit (STK)~\cite{STK:2025}. The work was carried out using \texttt{STK 12.10} locally on a Razer Blade 14 (Windows 11) machine. The plots were generated using the \texttt{Report \& Graph Manager} toolbox included in the STK package.

\section{Mission overview}

Operation Midnight Hammer (OMH) was a United States-led military strike on Iranian nuclear facilities, executed by the United States Air Force and Navy under the command of United States Central Command. The overall objective of OMH was to ``severely degrade Iran's nuclear weapon infrastructure", according to General John Daniel Caine, chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The operation was carried out on the 21st and 22nd June 2025 over 37 hours, with most strikes occurring between 02:10 and 02:30 Iran Standard Time on June 22nd (18:40-19:00 EDT on June 21st), as part of the broader Iran-Israel war that began with Israeli strikes on 13 June 2025. The strikes were authorised by President Donald Trump following failed diplomatic efforts, including a proposed 60-day negotiation period announced in February 2025, and amid International Atomic Energy Agency concerns over Iran's uranium enrichment to 60 per cent purity (not far from weapons grade). This marked the first United States offensive action in this conflict and the first attack on Iranian territory since 1988.

The mission involved 13 B-2 Spirit stealth bombers supported by 112 US aircraft, one US nuclear submarine, and a total of 75 surface-to-surface Tomahawk missiles, 14 GBU-57A/B Massive Ordnance Penetrator (MOP) bombs, and several air-to-surface weapons~\cite{lemay2025midnight}. A central component contributing to the success of the mission was space intelligence, which involved the use of the SBRIS constellation for real-time missile launch detection and cueing, the GPS constellation for navigation and tracking of aircraft and weapons, and several electro-optical LEO satellites for post-strike battle damage assessment (BDA). 

The mission unfolded as follows: at 04:00:00 UTC (00:00 EDT) of January 21st 2025, two packages of B-2 bombers took off from the 509th Bomb Wing at Whiteman Air Force Base, MO. The first package, acting as a decoy, comprised 6 aircraft and headed west to Oklahoma, California, Hawaii, and finally Guam. Its sole objective was to deceive enemy intelligence. The real operational package, comprising 7 B-2 units (each manned by two pilots), headed east on an 18-hour trajectory towards Iran. It traversed the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea, performing a total of 3 mid-air refuellings over international waters enabled by 12 KC-135 Stratotankers. Upon entering Israeli airspace, the seven B-2 aircraft were joined by a support package comprising F-35s, F-22s, and other aircraft to aid the strike operations by neutralising potential Iranian countermeasures (no firings were reported). At roughly 21:00:00 UTC (17:00 EDT), a US submarine stationed in the Persian Gulf fired a total of 75 sub-orbital Tomahawk missiles directed at the Isfahan Nuclear Technology Centre, coordinating the strike with the bombers and attack package. With the Tomahawks still soaring through the sky, at roughly 22:00:00 UTC, the joint flight formation entered Iranian airspace through the Iraqi border. At 22:40:00 UTC (18:40 EDT) on June 21st 2025, the lead B-2 bomber, codenamed Mytee-11, released the first two GBU-57s on the Fordow facility, closely followed by another 10 MOPs plus an additional 2 released on Natanz (2 per aircraft). Simultaneously, all GBU-57 warheads and Tomahawk missiles hit their intended targets, in a window of roughly 20 minutes. By 23:30:00 UTC (19::30 EDT), the B-2 bombers had left Iranian airspace, completely undetected. The aircraft returned to Whiteman AFB from their direction of origin, aided by multiple aerial refuellings, arriving at around 18:30:00 UTC (the total flight time was 37 hours). \subsection{Mission Concept Overview}

The mission involved   Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri, supported by over 125 aircraft, including F-35 Lightning II and F-22 Raptor fighters for suppression of enemy air defences, refuelling tankers, and intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance platforms. A United States Navy submarine, likely the USS Georgia, launched Tomahawk cruise missiles. To maintain surprise, six additional B-2s were deployed as decoys on a westward path to Guam.
The major phases of the mission proceeded as follows. The launch phase involved the B-2 bombers departing Whiteman Air Force Base eastward towards the Middle East, preceded by fighter escorts to neutralise potential surface-to-air missile threats. During the cruise phase, the bombers flew for 18 hours with three mid-air refuellings, following great circle routes over the Atlantic and into the region, minimising communication to preserve stealth. In the insertion phase, the aircraft entered Iranian airspace undetected, leveraging prior Israeli degradation of Iranian defences; routes reportedly crossed over Lebanon, Syria, and Iraq. The operations phase saw six B-2s targeting Fordow's ventilation shafts with 12 MOP bombs in sequence, while the seventh struck Natanz with two MOPs; the submarine fired 30 Tomahawks at Natanz and Isfahan, completing the strikes in approximately 30 minutes. Finally, the return phase included the bombers refuelling multiple times and returning to Whiteman Air Force Base, completing the round trip in 37 hours.
Key milestones included the bombers' takeoff on 21 June, the execution of strikes at 02:10 IRST on 22 June, and the subsequent announcement of a ceasefire on 23 June, which ended the war. The operation demonstrated advanced aerospace dynamics, including high-altitude bomber propagation and missile trajectories suitable for modelling in Systems Tool Kit (STK).
